Executive Strategic Abstract

This report analyzes the feasibility of introducing an 'End-to-End Turnkey' parking management model to the Johor market. Benchmarking against mature outsourcing models in ANZ and the UK, and leveraging local 'Compute Capital' advantages, we propose a digitization pathway based on the BOO (Build-Own-Operate) model to address fiscal leakage and optimize urban spatial governance.

Analyzing structural inefficiencies and the marginal benefit of digital intervention given Johor Bahru's (JB) unique geo-economic characteristics.

Structural Inefficiencies

Informal Economy Rent-Seeking

The 'Jaga Kereta' phenomenon is essentially the appropriation of public resources by the informal economy. This leads to direct municipal revenue leakage and disrupts the 'Pay-for-Service' social contract, causing market pricing mechanism failure.

Cross-Border Enforcement Arbitrage

While Singaporean vehicles possess high spending power, they effectively enjoy 'parking immunity' due to the lack of Cross-border Data Protocols. This constitutes regulatory arbitrage, reducing turnover in high-value zones.

Technological Intervention Architecture

Sovereign Edge Computing

Deploying localized LPR algorithm nodes for Inference at the Edge. This circumvents legal risks associated with cross-border data sovereignty and resolves latency issues in identifying foreign vehicles.

BOO Capital Structure

Adopting the Build-Own-Operate model. We internalize CapEx (equipment depreciation) and convert the government's OpEx (labor costs) into variable costs based on revenue sharing. This significantly improves the municipal balance sheet.
